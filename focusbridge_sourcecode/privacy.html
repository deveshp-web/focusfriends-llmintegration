The short version, for parents

  • Your child can use the app with no account and no sign-up.
  • We don't ask for your child's name. A first name is optional, and if entered it stays on your device.
  • Your child's activity — tasks, feelings, stars, badges — is stored on your own device and is not sent to us.
  • You can export or erase everything at any time in the Grown-ups Area.
  • No advertising, no ad networks, and no behavioral profiling.
  • We never sell data. We don't build profiles of children.

The one thing we do count: anonymous visit totals, so we know how many people use the app. No names, no accounts, no cookies, and nothing that identifies a person or a child.

Full policy

1. Who this is for

Focus Bridge is an educational tool for young children, including special-needs elementary students. It can be used at home by a family, or in a classroom under the direction of a school and teacher. We designed it to collect as little personal information as possible.

2. Children's data (COPPA)

Focus Bridge is intended for children, so we follow the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA). We do not collect personal information from a child under 13 without a proper consent basis:

  • School-authorized use: when a school directs use of Focus Bridge for a legitimate educational purpose, the school may provide consent consistent with COPPA's school-consent provisions. Child data is used only for that educational purpose and only as the school directs.
  • Direct-to-parent use: where a school is not involved and personal information would be collected, we obtain verifiable parental consent first. [DESCRIBE YOUR VERIFICATION METHOD.]

3. Home mode (no account, on-device)

Home mode requires no account, no school, and no sign-up. The app does not ask for a child's name (an optional first name may be entered and is kept only on the device), and it does not transmit a child's information to us — activity is stored locally in the app on the family's own device. A grown-ups gate protects the settings area, and a parent can export or erase all local data at any time.

4. Anonymous usage counts

To understand overall usage, the app may report aggregate, cookie-free counts to a privacy-focused analytics service (GoatCounter). These are simple tallies — for example, a page visit, or that a play session started. They contain no accounts, names, class codes, device identifiers, or per-child profiles, and are never used for advertising or behavioral targeting. [Intended to fit COPPA's "support for internal operations" exception — counsel to confirm.]

5. Student records and schools (FERPA)

When we work with a school, student information may constitute an education record under FERPA. In that role we act as a school official with a legitimate educational interest, under the school's direction and a Data Processing Agreement. We do not use education records for any purpose other than providing the service to the school.

6. What we collect

  • Provided at sign-up: a student's first name only (or an ID instead, at the school's choice) and a chosen avatar; a teacher's name and username for classroom accounts (no email required). We do not ask for or store last names.
  • Generated through use: task completion, emotion check-ins, rewards and badges, activity timestamps, and teacher notes. In Home mode this stays on the device.
  • Optional region sharing (parents): a parent may choose, in the Grown-ups Area, to share the first 3 digits of their ZIP code. This indicates a general region only — never a street, town, or anything about the child — and is sent as an anonymous count with no name or identifier attached. It is entirely optional and never required to use the app.
  • From teachers (adults) at classroom signup: school name and 5-digit school ZIP code. These describe a workplace, not a child or a home. They are used only to report aggregate usage (for example, how many schools or regions use the app), are never shown to students or families, are never sold, and are never used for advertising.
  • We do not collect home address, phone number, precise location, photographs, or biometric data.

7. How we use information

Only to operate Focus Bridge: show schedules and activities, and track progress for teachers and parents. We do not sell personal information, and we do not use it for advertising or behavioral profiling.

8. How information is shared

In Home mode, nothing is shared — the data does not leave the device. In classroom mode, information is available to the student's teacher and the school operating the classroom, and to our database provider strictly to run the service. We also disclose information if required by law. [CONFIRM SUB-PROCESSORS.]

9. Storage, security, and retention

Home-mode data is stored in the app on the family's device. Classroom data is stored with our database provider in [REGION], with access controls so that teachers reach only their own classrooms and students reach only their own records. We retain classroom data for [RETENTION PERIOD] and then delete it, or sooner on request. No method of storage is perfectly secure; we cannot guarantee absolute security.

10. Rights of parents and schools

Parents and schools may review a child's information, request corrections, request deletion, and withdraw consent (which stops further collection). In Home mode a parent can do all of this directly in the Grown-ups Area. For classroom data, contact [CONTACT]; we respond within [TIMEFRAME].

11. Data deletion

A parent can erase all local data from the Grown-ups Area. A teacher can delete a student's record from the dashboard, a student can erase their own data in the app (behind a grown-up check), and a teacher can permanently delete their own account and every classroom it owns from the dashboard's Settings tab. Deletion removes the record and associated logs.
